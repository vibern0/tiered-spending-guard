# Security-core call graph

## Ordinary scenario one-action executor

The pre-repair ordinary-Safe matrix uses one coordinator invocation per process. `review` reconstructs the public proposal and exposes no signing or broadcast capability. `submit-one` validates the exact proposal and opaque approval, completes a current-head public preflight, then lazily supplies only the signer capabilities required by the next closed action. `resume-one` accepts only an existing `outer-pending` record and disables signing and broadcast while it reconciles the journaled hash.

Safe signature construction is guard-specific and primary-first. The primary contract slot always precedes the contract secondary even when address sorting would reverse them. The ECDSA secondary remains a terminal typed extension produced through the frozen EIP-1193 typed-data facade. Signing and success use current-head evidence; historical early rejection uses the confirmed queue receipt block; confirmation uses receipt-poststate evidence. Alert-producing confirmation remains pending until independent sender delivery and receiver acknowledgement bind the same transaction and payload hashes.

The closed USDC period successor reuses that mechanical path through `createUsdcSuccessorExecutionView` and `executeUsdcSuccessorAction`. Its execution view contains only the three confirmed native-recovery descriptors needed to resolve the exact terminal queue state plus the distinct 19 `usdc-successor-*` descriptors; the frozen old `usdc-*` tail is never in the approval binding or next-action index. One atomic journal keeps the original 19 records, complete 22-slot recovery attachment, first 13 successful live entries, and the `usdcSuccessor` sibling. Each load and save revalidates the immutable source hash, exact headers, ordered commitments, unique live suffix, monotonic statuses, and durable receipt bytes. The first action depends only on the confirmed `usdc-funding` record and `native-queue-ready-replacement-execute`; replay resolves only `usdc-successor-base-x`, and queue lookups require one exact successor nonce/fingerprint.

Before a fresh successor action reaches materialization or signing, the coordinator separately compares the unchanged raw native tuple `(1793145600,10000000000000,30000000000000)` and the timestamp-derived approved effective period, validates the action's raw USDC window, applies the bound scenario/private admission budget, and rechecks the same state plus broadcast admission before outer signing. Both fresh reads require the funder's current `latest` and `pending` transaction counts to agree and require that agreed count to equal the nonce at the selected canonical block; that agreed value is the frozen outer nonce. The live submission verifier repeats `latest`/`pending` agreement before outer signing. Archive receipt-poststate reads and the historical queue-receipt `eth_call` path retain their original block nonce and do not acquire fresh-submission gates. The production live context likewise reconciles every durable historical receipt and both sides of its six-confirmation block-hash check through the fixed archive client; live-context nonce, balance, signer/runtime verification, and the sole `sendRawTransaction` edge remain on PublicNode. The fresh-successor adapter's separately fixed Thirdweb client continues to provide its current-state and simulation reads. The first successful USDC receipt establishes the approved period; later receipts and counters must remain in it. At or after the exclusive deadline, fresh work stops before private callbacks, while an exact durable hash remains eligible for keyless receipt reconciliation. For a mined successor hash, the adapter first binds the exact canonical receipt and outer envelope and reads canonical poststate at that receipt block. Before matrix-effect or observer confirmation, the coordinator persists successor-only `receiptInclusion` evidence containing that receipt's transaction hash, block number/hash, canonical block timestamp, and derived period. The atomic codec binds those fields to the receipt and the monotonic store permits their first write but rejects alteration or deletion. A receipt included in another period preserves the same inclusion evidence with terminal `stopped` status even when alert acknowledgement is absent; it cannot become matrix-success evidence or another attempt. Only an inclusion satisfying `effectivePeriod <= blockTimestamp < deadline` proceeds to the unchanged full effect, counter, queue, and observer confirmation. Shared confirmation requires the successor's nonempty observer `readyAt` and `expiresAt` values exactly; it never falls back to the historical receiver defaults.

This document records the primary-owner/guard-secondary topology and execution paths. The repository remains a testnet security prototype; the planner is unsigned and the verifier must pass before funds are deposited.

## Topology authority

One asset-holding Safe has exactly one owner: the configured primary passkey, with threshold 1 and no fallback handler. Contract and ECDSA secondary signers are guard-only identities; the guard verifies their exact-transaction approvals independently. The same TieredSpendingGuard occupies both Safe transaction-guard and module-guard slots. Zodiac Delay is the only Safe module. Delay has the Safe as owner, avatar, and target, and the Safe is its only upstream module. No second Safe, recovery signer, module, or custody account is created.

Two explicit secondary profiles share that topology. Historical ordinary-Safe plans with `secondaryProfile` omitted (or `dual`) keep both the enabled Safe-contract secondary and enabled ECDSA-extension secondary. Sepolia PWA Phase One sets `secondaryProfile: contract-only`: the Safe-contract secondary is nonzero and enabled, while the compatibility ECDSA address is `address(0)` and its guard tuple is exactly `(address(0), EcdsaExtension, false)`. Any terminal ECDSA extension therefore fails closed before signature recovery. The PWA profile does not add an owner, fallback handler, module, call type, or repair path; signer repair/recovery is deferred to Phase Two.

The primary contract-signature slot must come first, including when a contract secondary has a numerically lower address. Only that first slot authorizes Safe execution. The guard revalidates the primary via Safe and the secondary via ERC-1271 over the identical Safe transaction hash. ECDSA approvals retain the exact-hash terminal envelope. Primary rotation swaps the sole owner and guard identity atomically through Delay; secondary rotation never changes Safe ownership. ERC-4337 remains gated on live ordinary-path evidence and a restricted-fallback design review.

`buildVaultPlan` never signs, broadcasts, invents registry addresses, or accepts unverified infrastructure evidence. It requires a resolver-branded deployment infrastructure object, the exact observed deployer nonce, expected runtime code hashes for the four prerequisites, Safe proxy creation code, the deterministic four-prerequisite CREATE sequence, and a policy Delay address that matches that sequence. The selected atomic path uses `SafeAtomicSetupHelper` as the one Safe setup delegatecall target; the helper is stateless and derives the final Safe as `address(this)` so the initializer does not embed the Safe address. The production plan derives the Safe proxy address from the Safe factory, singleton, proxy creation code, initializer, and salt, and rejects a policy Safe that differs. The production plan emits unsigned transactions only: setup helper, guard, Delay, maintenance, then Safe proxy factory creation.

The Sepolia PWA provisioning path adds a separate resolver brand for two fresh credentials whose signer addresses are predicted by the already runtime-verified official Safe WebAuthn factory but have no code yet. The resolver reads the verified factory `SINGLETON()`, anchors packaged `SafeWebAuthnSignerProxy` creation code to `0x24eb5cf1cb02e3bc5ef6917bcd59e2cb1a7f02b257284dc89daeac1c37dada50`, builds each credential-specific constructor, and obtains each distinct expected runtime hash through read-only EVM creation simulation. `createPwaProvisioningPlan` admits only that brand plus opaque reviewed artifact evidence and the committed fixed USDC profile. The two signer calls are an explicit nonce prefix; the ordinary deployed-signer planner behavior is unchanged. The primary is the sole Safe owner, the secondary is guard-only, ECDSA/fallback are zero, and Delay remains the only module.

4. Delay owner/avatar/target are the Safe.

## Instant owner path

`Safe.execTransaction` → transaction guard `TieredSpendingGuard.checkTransaction` → exact Safe signature and policy check → Safe target call → `checkAfterExecution`.

The offchain signer path is provider-neutral: the passkey adapter accepts only the resolver-branded aggregate returned by the official deployment-verification boundary, selects its factory-bound `passkeySigner` instance record, pins provider chain identity before and after signing, and emits canonical Safe contract-signature slots only after an `eth_call` to the exact Safe-native passkey/ERC-1271 verifier identity. A caller cannot substitute `evidence: "verified"` or an arbitrary runtime hash because cloned/unbranded aggregates fail at the signer boundary. The same Safe-contract signer format is used for the configured primary passkey and the safe-contract secondary when each role is selected. The historical dual profile also permits the EIP-1193 secondary adapter, which uses `eth_signTypedData_v4` only after checking the configured chain/account and recovers the exact SafeTx locally. Every request must use the canonical SafeTx domain keys, `SAFE_TX_TYPES`, `primaryType`, and message fields before any provider call. The ECDSA secondary extension is terminal and versioned; secondary-only EOA output is never sufficient because the guard first requires the configured primary passkey contract-signature slot. In the contract-only PWA profile the ECDSA tuple is disabled, so only the primary-plus-contract-secondary layout can satisfy step-up or delayed authorization.

Only native transfers and selected ERC-20 `transfer` calls are admitted. The guard rejects delegate calls, batches, approvals, Permit/Permit2, arbitrary messages, unknown calldata, and non-passkey transfer signatures. Failed execution rolls back the counter update.

## Delayed proposal and execution

`Safe.execTransaction` → transaction guard → `Delay.execTransactionFromModule(to,value,data,operation)` with the exact single queued tuple → Delay queue.

After cooldown and before expiration, an unprivileged relayer calls pinned Zodiac Delay v1.1.1 `executeNextTx(to,value,data,operation)` → Safe `execTransactionFromModule` → module guard `checkModuleTransaction` → exact native/ERC-20 transfer → `checkAfterModuleExecution`. The module guard admits only the configured Delay address and supported `CALL` transfer tuples; Delay owns cooldown, FIFO ordering, expiration, `skipExpired`, and nonce cancellation semantics.

## Cancellation and emergency freeze

The configured primary passkey plus exactly one configured secondary signer may submit the configured Delay `setTxNonce(uint256)` cancellation or the guard `freeze()` call. Queue builders expose the next nonce so callers can enumerate all ordered queue items invalidated by cancellation before signing.

Each asset has an explicit base-recipient mode. In `any-valid-address` mode, a primary-only native/ERC-20 transfer may use any nonzero recipient while retaining all exact calldata, signer, X/Y, counter, and `CALL` checks. Step-up and delayed transfers always require an exact onchain allowlisted recipient. Immediate `setAssetPolicy` is accepted only for an unset token or a numeric decrease/equal value with a recipient subset and a mode that does not broaden the base lane. Policy increases, recipient additions, and opening a previously allowlisted base lane are rejected on the owner path. `repairPolicy(bytes32,address,uint256,uint256,uint256,uint256,address[],uint8)` (`0x0a285a05`) is admitted only as an exact `CALL` execution from the configured Delay after the primary plus exactly one configured secondary queued the action; it requires the supplied policy hash to equal the current pre-mutation policy hash, so any intervening policy drift makes the queued repair stale and it reverts. Direct queued `repairSigner(uint8,address,address)` (`0x28033279`) calls to the guard are denied; signer repair is available only through `GuardReplacementMaintenance.replaceSigner` so primary rotation updates the sole Safe owner and guard atomically, while secondary rotation updates only the guard. Role `2` and every other role value revert. A queued `repairPolicy` change becomes effective only through the verified Delay module after cooldown. `setAssetPolicy(address,uint256,uint256,uint256,uint256,address[],uint8)` is `0xa4b48d5e`; the old six-argument setter remains compatibility-only and always selects the restrictive allowlisted base mode. Immediate Delay tightening uses `setTxCooldown(uint256)` (`0xebb2b4a2`) and `setTxExpiration(uint256)` (`0x9b56d5be`).

## Atomic dual-guard replacement

`Delay` -> Safe module execution with `DELEGATECALL` -> `GuardReplacementMaintenance.replaceGuards(address,address)` (`0x7ec60d4f`) or `replaceSigner(address,uint8,address,address,address,uint256,bytes)` (`0x2d72b26a`). `replaceGuards` accepts only deployed code whose runtime hash is the reviewed `TieredSpendingGuard` artifact hash `0x596a66022b0aa360ed7faa0ba2fa550480f6b750a3ccc44da125ff14eff38428`; it also checks both guard slots and requires the replacement guard to preserve the current Safe, Delay, 24-hour period, primary passkey, safe-contract secondary, and ECDSA secondary bindings before installation. This existing repair surface remains the historical dual-profile authority; PWA Phase One does not exercise or claim contract-only signer repair.

The module guard permits this one target, selector, and operation only when the caller module is the configured Delay. In Safe storage, the maintenance code verifies both current guard slots, verifies both interfaces on the replacement, sets a reentrancy lock, and performs exactly two Safe self-calls: `setGuard` and `setModuleGuard`. Any failure reverts the complete operation. No arbitrary target list, calldata batch, or general delegatecall is exposed.

Signer repair updates guard configuration; only primary repair swaps the sole Safe owner atomically. Primary repair requires the sentinel predecessor; secondary repair requires zero predecessor. In both cases, the existing sole owner must match the guard primary and threshold must equal one; replacement guards receive the same maintenance authorization before the old guard can be removed. Both currently installed guard slots and the old guard's Safe, Delay, and role-signer configuration are checked before rotation. Role 0 rotates the primary passkey and requires deployed replacement code plus an ERC-1271 response over the exact domain-separated passkey-replacement proof hash. Role 1 rotates one configured secondary: the safe-contract secondary requires the same deployed-code/ERC-1271 replacement proof, while the ECDSA secondary requires empty replacement proof bytes. Role 2 reverts. All other module addresses, targets, selectors, and operations are denied by the module guard.

The ordinary scenario review represents contract-secondary repair in four ordered stages: separately reviewed canonical signer-factory creation; proof-bound delayed rotation; an exact post-rotation queue attempt where the old secondary rejects and the new secondary succeeds; cancellation of that proof item before final freeze. The public replacement descriptor binds the official factory, P-256 coordinates, verifier bitmap, derived address, and undeployed status. Because the future WebAuthn assertion is part of maintenance calldata, Delay calldata, and the Safe hash, the collector records only the repair proof domain until that private assertion exists. It never invents a hashable repair Safe transaction. The maintenance contract's ERC-1271 check is not an onchain pin of the Safe passkey factory/runtime/verifier class, so those public component gates remain mandatory package and post-receipt checks.

## Forbidden paths

Direct owner transfers outside the configured base, step-up, or delayed lanes; unlisted elevated recipients; unlisted modules; a second enabled module; nonzero fallback handler; delegatecalls outside fixed maintenance; batches; approvals; Permit/Permit2; arbitrary messages; unknown calldata; unknown assets; malformed Delay calls; nonzero Safe gas/refunds; approved-hash authorization; and any missing or inconsistent verification read are rejected. Monitoring and relaying can observe or execute an already-delay-approved item, but neither is an authorizer.

## Hashless recovery evidence boundary

`scripts/live/ordinary-scenario-hashless-certificate.ts` is read-only and non-authorizing. It loads the source journal before any execution store acquires its writer lock, rejects an existing source lock or temporary replacement, records an opaque filesystem snapshot, and revalidates that snapshot immediately before certificate branding. The original full-journal SHA-256 remains provenance for the interrupted attempt; the acceptance invariant is the canonical identity header plus `actions.slice(0, 19)` and `liveJournal.entries.slice(0, 10)`, so later append-only continuation records and live entries do not rewrite the historical hash. A caller-supplied clear-lock flag or structurally forged certificate has no effect.

The verifier binds the actual interruption revision and its executor/CLI/submission hashes separately from the later corrected revision, without attributing a failure cause. Two distinct public evidence providers must independently return the exact materialization block, historical certificate block, nonce-809 transaction and receipt, historical certificate-block nonce, latest/pending/current nonces, queue tuple and expiry, Safe/Delay nonces, balances, guard window, and raw spend-window counters. The historical certificate-block nonce remains fixed at 811. Later shared-funder activity may raise the current/latest/pending nonces and is classified as unrelated evidence, never as recovery authorization. The verifier separately pins a current block six confirmations behind two identical provider tips, reads the complete controlled state at that block, and canonically rereads the materialization, certificate, nonce-809 transaction, and current blocks after all state reads. It recomputes the materialization and queue fingerprints, requires the recovered nonce-809 sender and successful canonical receipt, and compares the complete provider views.
